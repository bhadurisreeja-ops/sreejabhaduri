Geo-Intelligence-Based Business Expansion, Customer Analytics & Logistics Optimization: A GeoAI Approach for Delhi

Python · GeoAI · Site-Suitability Modeling · Customer Segmentation · Route Optimization
  • Built a 3-module GeoAI decision-support prototype connecting restaurant site selection, customer analytics, and delivery-route planning for Delhi.
  • Composite site-suitability model (score range 0.25–0.72) combining road accessibility, POI proximity, and competitive density; top candidate scored 0.72, 34m from a main road with 2 malls and 2 metro stations within 500m.
  • Segmented 18,346 simulated customer orders from 4,772 unique customers into 5 spatial clusters; South Delhi (₹2.64M) and Connaught Place (₹2.23M) accounted for 50.6% of simulated order value, with peak demand at 8–9 PM.
  • Designed tiered 3km / 6km / 10km delivery service zones and an optimized multi-stop routing sequence.

02

Identification of Groundwater Potential Zones: Chitradurga District, Karnataka

ArcGIS Pro · AHP · Lineament Analysis · Machine Learning
  • Integrated 11 geo-environmental layers — elevation, slope, lineament density, LULC, NDVI, soil texture, aspect, infiltration, soil depth, curvature, and drainage density — using AHP weighting and weighted overlay to delineate groundwater potential zones.
  • Lineament rose diagram analysis revealed an even North-South / East-West fracture orientation (50% each), identifying fracture-intersection zones as priority well sites in the district's hard-rock terrain.
  • NDVI in high-vegetation zones ranged 0.264–0.549; soil depth mapping showed deep soils (up to ~200cm) dominating the district, indicating strong groundwater storage potential.
Group project · Team of 3 · Role: AHP weighting and layer analysis

03

Urban Blue Space Accessibility Mapping: Mumbai, Maharashtra

ArcPy · ArcGIS Pro
  • Automated an end-to-end ArcPy workflow — buffer analysis, Euclidean distance, zonal statistics, and reclassification — to assess population accessibility to water bodies across Mumbai City and Mumbai Suburban districts.
  • Zonal statistics: 1.54M residents within 300m (high accessibility), 2.83M within 500m (moderate), and 6.03M within 1000m (low-accessibility threshold) of a water body.
  • Reclassified distance surfaces into 4 accessibility classes, flagging the northeastern suburban core as the top-priority zone for blue-space infrastructure.

Urban Heat Island & Thermal Comfort Analysis Using LST, ATI & Land Cover Classification: Bengaluru District

Google Earth Engine · ArcGIS Pro · QGIS · MODIS/Landsat LST · NDVI · NDBI · ATI · UTFVI
  • Analyzed spatiotemporal LST across the 741 sq km Bengaluru district (population 18M+, density ~16,925/sq km) for 2010, 2015, 2020, and 2024.
  • Mean LST rose from 37.2°C (2010) to 42.6°C (2024) — a net warming of 5.4°C over 14 years; Extreme Urban Heat Island zones expanded from 0.07% to 15.86% of the district, while Cool Island coverage shrank from 9.28% to 1.67%.
  • Computed NDVI, NDBI, and Apparent Thermal Inertia (ATI), and applied the Urban Thermal Field Variance Index to classify the district into Comfortable, Moderate, and Critical thermal-stress zones.

05

Tourism Resource Mapping: Thiruvananthapuram District, Kerala

ArcGIS Pro · Kernel Density · Multi-Ring Buffer · LULC Classification · POI Analysis
  • Mapped the tourism resource base of Thiruvananthapuram district (8.52°N, 76.94°E), plotting 8 major attractions — Padmanabhaswamy Temple, Kovalam and Varkala beaches, Ponmudi hill station, and Neyyar Wildlife Sanctuary among them — as point features alongside hotel, restaurant, and transport amenities.
  • Kernel density analysis identified Padmanabhaswamy Temple, Napier Museum, Kovalam, and Varkala as the district's highest-density tourist hubs, with Veli and Shankumugham moderate and Neyyar Dam and the Ponmudi hills lowest.
  • Buffer analysis around attractions classified the district into High, Moderate, and Low service zones by hotel and restaurant density; multi-ring buffers from the primary road network at 0–2 km, 2–3 km, and >3 km set the corresponding accessibility tiers.
  • Cross-referencing with land use / land cover showed built-up area concentrated along the coast and southwest quadrant — coinciding with peak tourist activity — while nature-based sites sat in forest and wetland cover, flagging urban-growth pressure on those landscapes.
